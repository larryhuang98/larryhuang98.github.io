How the layers fit together +

qᵢ weights an atom-centered Gaussian. μᵢ is its covalent dipole, constructed as a sum of fixed uᵢ coefficients times the current CBV unit vectors; pᵢ is induced by the effective field of all other sites and any applied field. Both dipoles multiply derivatives of that Gaussian.

CBVs point from the selected atom to its listed neighbors. Using Wei’s convention, e²¹ points from atom 1 to atom 2. Dashed green arrows denote virtual covalent directions (such as H–H in water); no orthogonal frame is imposed. The model solves all atomic induced dipoles together. Their sum, the permanent atomic dipoles, and the charge-position terms give the molecular dipole. Contours show the signed charge density integrated along the viewing direction. βᵢ stays fixed as the molecule polarizes.
